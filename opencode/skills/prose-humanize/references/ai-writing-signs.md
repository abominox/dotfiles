# AI writing signs — reference taxonomy

Condensed from Wikipedia's **"Signs of AI writing"** (`WP:AISIGNS`), maintained
by WikiProject AI Cleanup. Source:
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing> — CC BY-SA 4.0.

The page's own framing, which matters more than the list:

> The patterns listed here are only potential signs of a problem, not the
> problem itself. Please do not merely treat these signs as the problems to be
> fixed; that could just make detection harder.

Use this to recognize a pattern, then fix the underlying emptiness. Do not
search-and-replace.

## Content

1. **Undue emphasis on significance** — *stands/serves as, is a testament to,
   a crucial/pivotal/vital role, underscores its importance, reflects broader,
   symbolizing its enduring, setting the stage for, represents a shift, key
   turning point, evolving landscape, indelible mark, deeply rooted.*
2. **Canned notability and media coverage** — *independent coverage, trade
   publications, featured in, written by a leading expert, active social media
   presence.*
3. **Superficial analysis via participial tail** — *highlighting, underscoring,
   emphasizing, ensuring, reflecting, symbolizing, contributing to, fostering,
   enhancing, valuable insights, resonate with.*
4. **Promotional register** — *boasts a, vibrant, rich, profound, showcasing,
   exemplifies, commitment to, nestled, in the heart of, groundbreaking,
   renowned, diverse array.*
5. **Vague connection** — *in connection with, associated with.*
6. **Vague attribution** — *industry reports, observers have cited, experts
   argue, some critics argue.*
7. **Outline-style "challenges and future prospects" close** — *Despite its
   ... faces several challenges, Despite these challenges, Future Outlook.*
8. **An "Awards and recognition" section.**

## Language

9. **AI vocabulary** — *additionally, align with, boasts, bolstered, crucial,
   deep dive, delve, emphasizing, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner,
   highlight (v.), interplay, intricate, landscape (abstract), meticulous,
   pivotal, robust, showcase, tapestry (abstract), testament, underscore (v.),
   valuable, vibrant.*
10. **Avoidance of plain copulatives** — *serves as / stands as / marks /
    functions as / represents; boasts / features / maintains / offers.*
11. **Negative parallelism** — three named subtypes: *Not just X, but also Y*;
    *Not X, but Y* ("It's not..., it's...", "no X, no Y, just Z"); the reversed
    *Y rather than X*.
12. **Lead sentences that treat a broad title as a proper noun.**
13. **Rule of three** — triads of adjectives or short phrases, used to make
    thin analysis look thorough.

## Style

14. Title Case headings.
15. Overuse of boldface.
16. **Inline-header vertical lists** — `- **Term**: explanation`.
17. Heading errors: H1 overuse, skipped levels, headings containing only
    headings.
18. Emoji as formatting.
19. Unusual tables.
20. Curly quotes and apostrophes.
21. `<mark>` highlighting.

## Chat residue

22. *I hope this helps, Of course!, Certainly!, You're absolutely right!,
    Would you like me to..., let me know.*
23. **Hedging about sources** — *while specific details are limited, not widely
    documented, in the provided sources, based on available information.*
24. Phrasal templates and placeholder text.

## Signs of human writing (the inverse, more useful for a rewrite)

- Simple *is* / *has* sentences.
- Plain verbs over stiff synonyms: *wrote* not *authored*, *used* not
  *utilized*, *died* not *passed away*.
- Superlatives and definites: *one of the best*, *the only*.
- Qualifiers and intensifiers used naturally: *very, perhaps, tends to*.
- Isolated wordiness: *as a result of, in order to, the fact that*.

## Indicators the page explicitly calls ineffective

Do not build rules on these — they do not discriminate:

- Perfect grammar.
- Mixed casual/formal register.
- Prose that merely *feels* bland or robotic.
- "Fancy" or academic prose in general.
- **Transition words in isolation.** "Additionally," or "Moreover," alone is a
  weak signal. Flag high density, not individual instances.

## Status note on em dashes

Overuse of em dashes was demoted to a **historical** indicator (2022–September
2026). OpenAI's GPT-5.1 suppressed them, and a July 2026 study found that
among contemporary models only Claude used them above professional-writer
rates. Treat a hard failure on `—` as obsolete. The page notes, uncited, that
the residue — comma-and-period monotony — is now itself a sign.

Also demoted to historical: knowledge-cutoff statements, section summaries,
prompt refusals, lexical diversity.
